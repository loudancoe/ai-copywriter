# Google AI Content Guidance

## How to use this reference

Use this file when creating, editing, or reviewing website content produced with AI or other forms of automation.

Suggested request:

> Check this content against **Google AI Content Guidance**. Identify anything that does not comply with the guidance, explain why, and provide corrected wording where needed. Do not add unsupported SEO, AEO, or GEO recommendations.

When reviewing content, assess every item in the review checklist and distinguish between:

- Requirements or recommendations supported by the source.
- Practical suggestions that go beyond the source.
- Claims that cannot be verified from the source.

## Purpose

The objective is to produce original, high-quality, people-first content that may perform well in Google Search without violating Google’s spam policies.

> Source: [Google Search’s guidance about AI-generated content](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/02/google-search-and-ai-content), published 8 February 2023.

## Scope and limitations

This guidance concerns AI-generated content appearing in Google Search.

It does not provide specific recommendations for:

- Being cited or surfaced in AI Overviews or other AI-generated answers.
- AEO or GEO optimisation.
- Page structure, headings, schema markup, metadata, internal linking, or technical SEO.
- Particular AI tools, models, prompts, or production workflows.
- Minimum or maximum content lengths.
- Whether citations are a direct ranking factor.

Do not present unsupported AEO, GEO, formatting, or technical recommendations as though they came from this source.

## Core principles

- Prioritise content quality over the method used to create it.
- Create content primarily for people rather than to influence search rankings.
- Produce content that is original, useful, helpful, and reliable.
- Demonstrate relevant elements of E-E-A-T:
  - Experience
  - Expertise
  - Authoritativeness
  - Trustworthiness
- Apply the same quality standards to human-written, AI-assisted, and automatically generated content.
- Do not assume that using AI provides a ranking advantage or disadvantage by itself.
- Judge the finished content on its value to users, not simply on whether AI was involved.

## Appropriate use of AI and automation

AI or automation may be used when it helps produce useful and original content.

Acceptable uses may include:

- Assisting a human creator during content production.
- Generating information that is genuinely helpful to users.
- Supporting new forms of expression or creativity.
- Producing suitable automated content, such as scores, forecasts, or transcripts.

Before using AI, establish how it will improve the usefulness or originality of the finished content.

AI use is not automatically considered spam. The purpose, quality, and usefulness of the resulting content are what matter.

## Content creation guidelines

For every piece of content:

1. Define the intended audience and the genuine need the content will meet.
2. Confirm that the primary purpose is helping that audience.
3. Add original value rather than producing another version of existing material without meaningful improvement.
4. Review the content for usefulness, reliability, and overall quality.
5. Check whether the content demonstrates suitable experience, expertise, authoritativeness, and trustworthiness.
6. Consider the content’s **Who, How, and Why**:
   - **Who:** Make it clear who created the content when readers would reasonably want to know.
   - **How:** Explain how the content was created when readers would reasonably expect that information.
   - **Why:** Ensure the reason for creating the content is to help people, not primarily to manipulate search rankings.
7. Apply human editorial judgement before publication, particularly where reliability is important.

## Authors and bylines

- Include an accurate author byline when readers would reasonably expect to know who wrote the content.
- Do not add bylines indiscriminately where authorship would not normally matter.
- Publishers appearing in Google News should follow Google News requirements for bylines and author information.
- Do not list an AI system as the author.
- If AI materially contributed to the content, explain its role separately when that information would be useful or reasonably expected.

## AI and automation disclosures

Consider adding a disclosure when a reader might reasonably ask how the content was created.

A disclosure should:

- Clearly explain the relevant role of AI or automation.
- Help readers understand the production process.
- Avoid implying that the AI system is personally responsible for the content.

The source does not require an AI disclosure on every piece of AI-assisted content.

## Authority, reliability, and sensitive topics

- Use reliable information, regardless of whether the content is produced by a person or with AI.
- Apply a particularly high reliability standard to topics where incorrect information could cause significant harm or mislead users.
- Take additional care with:
  - Health information
  - Financial information
  - Civic information
  - Other topics where information quality is critically important
- Review claims that contradict well-established consensus with particular care.

Google states that its systems place greater emphasis on reliability signals for these topics. The source does not prescribe a specific citation method or claim that adding citations alone improves rankings.

## Things to avoid

Do not:

- Use AI or automation primarily to manipulate Google Search rankings.
- Treat AI as a cheap or easy method of gaming search results.
- Generate large quantities of content solely to attract search traffic.
- Publish low-quality content simply because it can be produced quickly.
- Assume AI-generated content receives special ranking benefits.
- Assume all AI-generated content is automatically prohibited.
- List an AI tool as the author instead of identifying the responsible person or publisher.
- Add unsupported claims about AEO, GEO, AI citations, schema, or technical optimisation to this guidance.

Using automation primarily to manipulate search rankings violates Google’s spam policies.

## Supported conclusions

The source supports the following conclusions:

- AI-generated content is not automatically against Google’s guidelines.
- AI can be used to create useful and original content.
- Content quality matters more than whether AI was used.
- Google seeks to reward original, helpful, reliable, people-first content.
- E-E-A-T remains relevant to AI-assisted content.
- AI does not provide an inherent ranking advantage.
- Manipulative automated content can violate Google’s spam policies.
- Accurate author information and appropriate production disclosures can help readers understand who created content and how it was produced.
- Reliability is especially important for health, financial, civic, and similarly sensitive topics.

## Claims not supported by this source

Do not infer from this guidance that:

- AI-written content will rank better or worse solely because AI was used.
- AI content must always include a disclosure.
- Every page must have an author byline.
- A particular percentage of a page must be written by a human.
- AI content can rank without being original, useful, or reliable.
- Adding citations, schema, FAQs, headings, or particular formatting guarantees improved visibility.
- The guidance explains how to earn citations in AI-generated answers.
- Passing an AI-content detector is necessary for search performance.
- Google requires publishers to avoid AI-generated content entirely.

## Review checklist

Before publishing, confirm that:

- [ ] The content is intended primarily to help people.
- [ ] The content serves a clear audience need.
- [ ] The content provides original or meaningfully useful value.
- [ ] The finished work is high quality and reliable.
- [ ] Relevant experience or expertise is evident.
- [ ] The publisher or creator is trustworthy and appropriately identified.
- [ ] An accurate author byline is included where readers would reasonably expect one.
- [ ] Any material use of AI or automation is explained where readers would reasonably expect that information.
- [ ] AI is not presented as the author.
- [ ] Sensitive claims have received an appropriately rigorous reliability review.
- [ ] The content does not contradict well-established consensus without careful substantiation.
- [ ] AI or automation was not used primarily to manipulate search rankings.
- [ ] No unsupported claims about AI visibility, AEO, GEO, citations, or ranking guarantees have been added.
